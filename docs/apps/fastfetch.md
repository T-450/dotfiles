# fastfetch

System summary in three labelled groups beside a λ drawn in the palette's colors, shown by Ghostty through the kitty graphics protocol.

The summary follows the `groups` preset of [LierB/fastfetch](https://github.com/LierB/fastfetch): `OS`, `WM` and `PC` are bold labels, each with its members on a tree under it, and every member has a Nerd Font icon in place of a word (Material Design glyphs, `nf-md-*`, named above each key in `config.jsonc.tmpl`). A group's label and icons share one syntax color (`syn_keyword`, `syn_constant`, `syn_special`: almond, mint and honey on Colibri). The tree and the bars' tracks are the border tone (`border_inactive`), a label's own value is bright (`fg_bright`) and the other values are the text color. Memory, disk and battery show one bar in the status colors (success, warning, error) plus their numbers, and the last row is the palette's swatches. The `WM` group ends with the active palette's name, a row that always prints, so the tree stays closed in a terminal whose font fastfetch cannot read. On the T14 the widest line ends at column 73, inside a half-screen terminal's 81; a narrower window clips lines (`disableLinewrap`) instead of wrapping them under the logo.

The logo is `logo.svg.tmpl`, rendered by `theme` like any other template: the λ as an isometric line drawing (30 degree axes, its face extruded toward the lower left, a dot grid on the front face) printed straight on the terminal's background. The ink is `fg_bright` pushed a third of the way to `accent` (lilac-white on Colibri) and looks soaked in: a slightly bled, wavering edge, a faint halo and alpha that thins unevenly. Callouts in `ansi1` (apricot on Colibri) point at its parts on dashed leader lines, each disc marked with a sigil (a triangle, a crosshair, a cut diamond, a hexagon), and the machine's name, T450, stands over it in squared block letters drawn the same way as the λ (same projection, extrusion, ink and dot grid), as paths, so no font is needed. The drawing stays inside the summary's rows. `render.sh` (run by `theme`) rasterizes it with `rsvg-convert` to `logo.png` at twice the size it is drawn at, so `theme set` recolors the logo for the next run. When Ghostty scales the picture, the color of a nearly transparent pixel bleeds into its neighbors, so `render.sh` also cuts the faintest 2% of alpha and gives the transparent pixels the terminal's background color: as rasterized, the faintest pixels hold pure white or green (a color at alpha 1 has only all-or-nothing channels left) and showed as specks.

The logo type is `kitty-direct`: Ghostty reads the PNG and fits it to the logo's 34x22 cells, the summary's 20 rows with a blank row above and below, so the λ stands on the same rows in every window, from the first row's cap line to the last row's baseline. Ghostty scales the picture down smoothly (it is sharp up to a display scale of 2), but it also stretches it to the cell box, so the λ is drawn for cells of 10x22.5: Lilex's are 10x22 px at 12 pt and 10x23 px at 13 pt, and the middle keeps the stretch at 2% either way. A font with other proportions needs a new canvas height in `logo.svg.tmpl`. Plain `kitty` has fastfetch scale the image itself and keep its shape, but from the window's pixel size per cell, which Ghostty reports with the window's leftover padding included (23.5 px rows instead of 23): the picture then covers part of one more row than fastfetch counts, and the summary starts a row lower in some windows and not in others. Only `config.jsonc` and `logo.png` are linked, so other presets in `~/.config/fastfetch/` stay yours.
