# swaybg

The wallpaper: the last pick from waypaper (super+w), else the palette's own, Neptune in ASCII characters on dark palettes and the plain `bg_desktop` color on light ones.

The image is [lyje9y on wallhaven](https://wallhaven.cc/w/lyje9y), uploaded by Jesseract. Its
original source and license are unknown, so the repo never holds it.
`pkg/wallpaper-neptune-ascii/PKGBUILD` downloads it, checks its SHA-256, and installs it to
`/usr/share/backgrounds/neptune-ascii.png`. `render.sh` scales it to 3840x2160, the largest output,
as `~/.config/swaybg/wallpaper.png`.

`swaybg.service` shows `~/.local/state/wallpaper/current` with `-m fill` over the palette's desktop
color, and `theme set` restarts it. That link is the current wallpaper: `wallpaper-set FILE`
points it at FILE and restarts swaybg ([waypaper](waypaper.md) runs it after each pick), and
`setup.sh` points it back at `wallpaper.png` when it is missing or its file is gone. It lives in
`~/.local/state`, outside the repo, so a pick survives logins and `theme set`.

The image passes the readability checks for every dark palette: the 15% of it that shows through
an unfocused window keeps `fg_muted` text at 4.5:1 or better. It suits none of the light palettes.

The wallpaper can be a video too. `wallpaper-set FILE` takes either; `wallpaper-is image|video`
reads the link's MIME type and is the `ExecCondition` of `swaybg.service` (an image) and
[gslapper](gslapper.md)`.service` (a video), so exactly one of them draws the desktop. waypaper lists
images only, so pick a video with `wallpaper-set` itself.

The lock screen has its own image, `~/.local/state/wallpaper/lock` ([hyprlock](hyprlock.md) can't
play video): `wallpaper-set --lock FILE` points it at an image, and `setup.sh` points it at the Blue
Dawn eclipse below when it is missing, else at the palette's `wallpaper.png`.

`pkg/wallpaper-cosmic-storm/PKGBUILD` makes Cosmic Storm Haze's moving wallpapers from two
Pinterest pins: a storm over a sea of clouds for the desktop and "Blue Dawn Wallpaper" (by Siren),
an eclipse over a cloud bowl, for the login screen, plus that eclipse as the lock image. Their
licenses are unknown, so, like Neptune, the clips are downloaded and checked against their SHA-256
and never committed. Both are portrait 720x1280 at 24 fps; `loop.sh` cuts a 16:9 band, cleans the
compression noise, upscales to 1920x1080 with every step at 16 bits per channel (no banding in the
dark gradients), and fades the last frames into the first, so playback restarts on the frame that
follows the last one: the seam measures like any other frame change (SSIM). They install to
`/usr/share/backgrounds/cosmic-storm/` as 10-bit HEVC, which the UHD 620 decodes in hardware. The
desktop loop is a pick: `wallpaper-set /usr/share/backgrounds/cosmic-storm/cosmic-storm.mp4`.
